\section{Evaluation Results}\label{sec:eval-results}

We evaluate the three headline analyses of Section~\ref{sec:compute}
against the standard tool for each task: \texttt{deconstruct} versus
\texttt{vg deconstruct}~\cite{garrison2018variation}, \texttt{growth} versus
Panacus~\cite{parmigiani2024panacus}, and \texttt{pav} versus
\texttt{odgi pav}~\cite{guarracino2022odgi}. All runs were performed on a single
node with an AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 9955WX (16 physical cores, 32 hardware
threads) and 503\,GiB of RAM. Each command was run at \textbf{1} and \textbf{16}
threads, one at a time, and wrapped in \texttt{/usr/bin/time\,-v} to record
wall-clock time and peak resident set size (RSS). \gfaz reads the compressed
\texttt{.gfaz} container directly; the baselines read the uncompressed GFA
(\texttt{odgi} additionally builds an \texttt{.og} index first, timed
separately).

The chr1 and chr6 graphs are human pangenome graphs with a linear CHM13
reference, used for \texttt{deconstruct} and \texttt{pav}; the \emph{E. coli}
graph is a 13-strain bacterial pangenome used for \texttt{growth}. (The bacterial
graph is circular and stores only W-line walks, so it is not a meaningful target
for reference-anchored \texttt{deconstruct} nor for \texttt{odgi pav}, which does
not load W-line paths; it is retained as a \texttt{growth} data point.)
The scaling experiments additionally use four whole-genome graphs: the three
HPRC releases (v1.1, v2.0, v2.1) and HGSVC3---an $80.1$\,GB Minigraph--Cactus
pangenome from a \emph{different} consortium (the Human Genome Structural
Variation Consortium; $65$ samples plus CHM13/GRCh38, $29{,}130$ walks,
$107.1$\,M nodes, compressed $28.1\times$ to a $2.85$\,GB container). HGSVC3
tests that the engine generalizes beyond a single construction pipeline, and
being smaller than the largest HPRC graphs it is one on which the baselines
still (barely) run, enabling a direct whole-genome comparison.

\subsection{Deconstruct (GFA\,$\rightarrow$\,VCF)}\label{subsec:eval-deconstruct}

Table~\ref{tab:eval-deconstruct} reports GFA\,$\rightarrow$\,VCF deconstruction
across six graphs---from the chr1/chr6 chromosomes to whole-genome
pangenomes---against \texttt{vg deconstruct}. Wherever \texttt{vg} runs, \gfaz
produces the same set of variant sites: record counts agree to within $0.13\%$ on
chr1/chr6 ($1{,}593{,}899$ vs.\ $1{,}593{,}956$ and $1{,}304{,}121$ vs.\
$1{,}302{,}385$) and to within $0.17\%$ on HGSVC3 ($25{,}323{,}695$ vs.\
$25{,}368{,}045$), consistent with the $\sim$$99.99\%$ position concordance
established in Section~\ref{subsec:ce-deconstruct}. On the chromosome graphs
\gfaz is $36$--$71\times$ faster with $3.3$--$3.4\times$ less memory, and the
output is thread-count invariant (byte-identical VCFs at 1 and 16 threads).

The gap widens at whole-genome scale, where \texttt{vg} must load the entire
uncompressed graph while \gfaz operates on the $2.2$--$4.8$\,GB container.
\texttt{vg} completes on HGSVC3 ($80$\,GB) only by consuming $397$\,GB of
RAM---nearly the whole node---and does not complete at all on the $358$/$369$\,GB
HPRC giants; on HGSVC3 \gfaz is $15.2\times$ faster with $7.6\times$ less memory.
\gfaz emits the genome-wide VCF ($23$--$36$\,M records) on every graph, finishing
the two largest in $\sim$$40$ minutes at 16 threads with $191$--$195$\,GB peak
RSS---about half the uncompressed GFA. Single-threaded runs on those two are
omitted as impractical ($>$10\,h extrapolated); on hprc-v1.1 the 1- vs.\
16-thread runs ($79.9$ vs.\ $5.1$\,min) show $15.6\times$ scaling. Because
deconstruction must hold per-site allele sequences, \gfaz's peak RSS here is
comparable to (not below) the uncompressed GFA; the lighter analyses in
Sections~\ref{subsec:eval-growth}--\ref{subsec:eval-pav} stay well under it.

\subsection{Pangenome Growth}\label{subsec:eval-growth}

Table~\ref{tab:eval-growth} compares \texttt{growth} against Panacus, the
state-of-the-art exact growth-curve tool, across all evaluation graphs. With
\gfaz configured to Panacus's grouping (\texttt{sample-hap-seq}), the two tools
produce the \emph{same} growth curve: at every growth point the \gfaz
expectation matches Panacus's reported value (e.g.\ chr1 at $k=100$:
$5{,}655{,}489$; at $k=1000$: $10{,}927{,}766$; identical full-pangenome totals
of $13{,}590{,}187$, $4{,}781{,}777$, and $819{,}657$ nodes for chr1, chr6, and
\emph{E. coli}). \gfaz computes this curve $15$--$25\times$ faster while using
$9$--$16\times$ less peak memory. Crucially, its peak RSS stays at a few hundred
megabytes---more than $10\times$ below the size of the uncompressed GFA---directly
demonstrating the sub-GFA memory thesis: the traversal store remains
grammar-compressed and only a compact coverage accumulator is added.

The lower half of Table~\ref{tab:eval-growth} extends \texttt{growth} to four
whole-genome graphs. Panacus completes on all of them, but its runtime and memory
track the uncompressed GFA: $23.8$\,min / $45.8$\,GB on hprc-v1.1, $40.6$\,min /
$71.1$\,GB on HGSVC3, and over four hours with $327$--$336$\,GB of RSS on the two
largest HPRC graphs. \gfaz computes the identical curve---matching Panacus's node
totals exactly at every growth point, on HGSVC3 as on the HPRC graphs---in
$6.2$--$41$\,s with only $6.3$--$13.7$\,GB of RSS, a $230$--$369\times$ speedup
with $7$--$25\times$ less memory and peak RSS about $27\times$ below the GFA
(HGSVC3 alone: $253\times$ at $8.8\times$ less memory). On hprc-v1.1 the \gfaz 1-
vs.\ 16-thread runs ($69.2$\,s vs.\ $6.2$\,s) give $11\times$ scaling.

\subsection{Presence/Absence Variation}\label{subsec:eval-pav}

Table~\ref{tab:eval-pav} compares \texttt{pav} against \texttt{odgi pav} on the
chr1 and chr6 graphs, computing the whole-path PAV matrix
($2{,}262$ and $1{,}410$ reference windows $\times$ $46$ sample groups). Both
tools emit a matrix with identical structure; \gfaz's PAV ratios reproduce
\texttt{odgi}'s reference semantics (Section~\ref{subsec:ce-instances}). The
performance gap is the largest of the three analyses: at 16 threads \gfaz is
$299\times$ (chr1) and $613\times$ (chr6) faster than \texttt{odgi pav} while
using $3.1$--$3.3\times$ less peak memory, and it operates directly on the
\texttt{.gfaz}, avoiding the \texttt{odgi build} step (a further $61$\,s / $31$\,s)
that \texttt{odgi pav} requires. Single-threaded \texttt{odgi pav} did not finish
within a day and is omitted; even at 16 (and, in prior runs, 32) threads it
remains three orders of magnitude slower than \gfaz, whose single-threaded run
already completes in under two minutes.

The lower half of Table~\ref{tab:eval-pav} extends \texttt{pav} to whole-genome
scale, where \gfaz again runs alone: \texttt{odgi} does not load W-line walks
(all whole-genome graphs use W-lines). On hprc-v1.1, \texttt{pav} over all
$30{,}640$ whole-walk windows completes in $1.8$\,min at 16 threads
($19.3$\,min at 1 thread, $10.7\times$ scaling) with $77$\,GB peak RSS; HGSVC3's
$29{,}130$ windows take $3.6$\,min / $126$\,GB. Unlike \texttt{deconstruct} and
\texttt{growth}, \texttt{pav} does \emph{not} keep its working set below the GFA:
the PAV node-set index and per-window accumulators are not grammar-compressed, so
memory grows with the number of windows and nodes---on HGSVC3 the $126$\,GB
working set already exceeds the $80$\,GB input. On the two largest HPRC graphs
($94{,}554$ and $60{,}807$ windows over $148$--$166$\,M nodes) it exceeds the
$503$\,GB node---both runs were terminated near $470$\,GB before completing. This
is the one analysis that does not yet meet the sub-GFA memory goal at
whole-genome scale; compressing the PAV index is the most pressing optimization
(its working set, not the traversal store, is the bottleneck).

\subsection*{Summary}

Across all three analyses, computing directly on the compressed container makes
\gfaz one to three orders of magnitude faster than the established tools---$15$--$71\times$
over \texttt{vg deconstruct}, $15$--$369\times$ over Panacus, and $299$--$613\times$
over \texttt{odgi pav}---and, for \texttt{deconstruct} and \texttt{growth}, keeps
peak RSS below the size of the uncompressed GFA. These gains hold across pangenome
families: on HGSVC3, a whole-genome graph from a different consortium, \gfaz
reproduces \texttt{vg} and Panacus outputs while running $15$--$253\times$ faster
with $7.6$--$8.8\times$ less memory---\texttt{vg deconstruct} alone needs $397$\,GB,
nearly the entire node. The same property lets \gfaz
scale to whole human pangenomes the baselines cannot practically handle: on the
$358$ and $369$\,GB HPRC graphs, \texttt{vg} and \texttt{odgi} cannot load the
input within memory, while Panacus \texttt{growth} completes only after
$\sim$$4$ hours and $\sim$$330$\,GB---whereas \gfaz runs \texttt{deconstruct} and
\texttt{growth} from a $4.5$\,GB container in tens of seconds to tens of minutes
with an order of magnitude less memory. The lone exception is whole-genome
\texttt{pav}, whose uncompressed working set still exceeds node memory on the two
largest graphs---the clearest target for the next round of optimization.
